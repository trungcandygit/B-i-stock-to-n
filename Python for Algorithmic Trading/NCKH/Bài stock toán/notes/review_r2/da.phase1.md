## Contract Paraphrase

D1 (methodology_rigor, mandatory, owner methodology). From an adversarial-challenge lens: the design, data handling, statistical reporting and reproducibility must be strong enough that the headline inferences cannot be explained away by a construction choice, a data artefact, or an unreported specification search. I do not score this dimension, but I will report any design choice that mechanically produces the result it is then said to discover.

D2 (domain_accuracy, mandatory, owner domain). The claims must match current domain evidence, prior work must be represented fairly, and domain terms and results must be used correctly. I do not score it; a misrepresented prior result that the argument leans on would still be reported as a finding.

D3 (argumentative_coherence, mandatory, owner da). The central thesis must be internally consistent; each step from assumption to evidence to conclusion must hold; and there must be no circular reasoning, tautology presented as discovery, non sequitur, or conclusion that outruns its evidence. This is the dimension I own and score.

D4 (cross_disciplinary_relevance, high, owner perspective). Framing, definitions and implications must be understandable and substantiated for adjacent-field readers. I do not score it; overreach in claimed implications is still reportable under D3.

D5 (writing_and_structure, normal, owner eic). Organisation, clarity, figure/table quality and venue conventions. I do not score it.

D6 (venue_fit_and_contribution, mandatory, owner eic). The manuscript must fit the venue and make an original, significant contribution. I do not score it; whether a stated contribution is substantively new or a restatement of a definition bears on D3 when the paper's argument depends on calling it new.

## Scoring Plan

### D3: argumentative_coherence
dimension_id: D3
what_to_look_for: Trace each headline claim in abstract, introduction and conclusion back to a stated assumption plus evidence; test whether any formal result or hypothesis is true by construction, whether a test reuses the inputs it is meant to validate, whether a reported dominance or decomposition is a restatement of a chosen parameter, whether null or weak results are reported as such, and whether alternative explanations are addressed.
what_triggers_block: A headline claim presented as an empirical or theoretical finding is shown by the manuscript's own definitions or tables to hold by construction or via circular validation, and the claim is central to the stated contribution, but the paper retains a non-circular core that could carry a reframed contribution after substantial rewriting or re-analysis.
what_triggers_warn: One or more claims in abstract, introduction or conclusion exceed what the reported evidence supports, a null or mixed result is framed more favourably than the numbers allow, or an evident alternative explanation is left unaddressed, while the core chain of argument still holds.
what_triggers_fatal: The manuscript's own definitions, equations or reported numbers directly contradict its central conclusion, or every substantive contribution reduces to a tautology or circular test so that no non-trivial claim survives revision.
