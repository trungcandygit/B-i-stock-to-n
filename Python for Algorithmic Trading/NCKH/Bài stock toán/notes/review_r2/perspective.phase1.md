## Contract Paraphrase

D1 (methodology_rigor, mandatory, owned by the methodology seat). The design, data handling, statistical reporting and reproducibility support must clear the normal peer-review bar of the field. As the practitioner seat I do not score this; I note only that, from my angle, rigor matters insofar as a risk desk or index committee could re-run the numbers and trust them.

D2 (domain_accuracy, mandatory, owned by the domain seat). Claims must match current domain evidence, prior work must be represented fairly, and terminology must be correct. I do not score it; from the practitioner side, mis-describing how index providers or risk vendors actually work would be the relevant failure, and I will report such observations as findings, not as a D2 score.

D3 (argumentative_coherence, mandatory, owned by the Devil's Advocate with the methodology seat eligible). The core thesis must be internally consistent and supported by evidence, without undermining fallacies. Not mine to score; logic and internal-consistency checks belong to other seats.

D4 (cross_disciplinary_relevance, high priority, owned by me). Framing, definitions and implications must be readable by adjacent-field readers (risk managers, index providers, regulators, econophysicists, applied econometricians), and any claim that reaches beyond the core discipline (practical, policy, cross-market) must be substantiated. This is where I judge decision relevance, transferability to other settings, materiality of reported effects relative to everyday practice, usability of the key deliverable, clarity of exhibits for non-specialists, and whether policy recommendations follow from the evidence.

D5 (writing_and_structure, normal, owned by the Journal-Fit seat). Organisation, exposition, figure and table quality and venue conventions. Not scored by me; I may raise exhibit-usability points for practitioners inside my D4 review.

D6 (venue_fit_and_contribution, mandatory, owned by the Journal-Fit seat). Fit to the configured venue and originality and significance of the contribution. Not scored by me; no venue binding is available for this run, so I make no venue-alignment claim.

## Scoring Plan

### D4: cross_disciplinary_relevance
dimension_id: D4
what_to_look_for: whether practitioners (risk managers, index providers, exchanges, regulators) can act on the results; whether the core deliverable is stated as a usable formula; whether the richer estimator adds decision value beyond the simpler benchmark; whether the analytical result transfers to other settings and is shown to; whether economic magnitudes are benchmarked against ordinary estimation error and checked out of sample; whether exhibits are readable by non-specialists; whether policy recommendations are supported by the evidence presented
what_triggers_block: the practical or policy implications rest on claims the evidence does not support (recommendations with no link to any reported result, or materiality asserted without any benchmark), so an adjacent-field reader would be misled about what to do
what_triggers_warn: implications are supported in direction but under-substantiated for practice, such as no out-of-sample or benchmark check of economic magnitude, transferability asserted but not demonstrated, the added value of the richer method over a simpler one left implicit, or key exhibits and formulas hard for practitioners to use
