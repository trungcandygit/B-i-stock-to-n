## Contract Paraphrase

D1 (methodology_rigor): From a practitioner's standpoint, this asks whether the empirical design, data treatment, and statistical reporting are sound enough that a risk desk could re-run the analysis and trust the result; it is owned by the methodology seat and I will not score it.

D2 (domain_accuracy): This asks whether claims match what the finance and econometrics literature actually establishes, with prior work and terminology represented correctly; it belongs to the domain seat and I will not score it.

D3 (argumentative_coherence): This asks whether the central thesis holds together internally and whether the evidence offered really supports the conclusions drawn, free of undermining fallacies; it is scored by the devil's advocate and methodology seats, not by me.

D4 (cross_disciplinary_relevance): This is my dimension. It asks whether a reader from portfolio risk management, index construction, or ETF product design could follow the framing and definitions without specialist econophysics training, and whether any claims about practical, regulatory, or cross-field implications are actually substantiated rather than asserted.

D5 (writing_and_structure): This asks whether the manuscript is organised clearly, whether exposition, figures, and tables communicate well, and whether venue conventions are followed; it is owned by the editor-in-chief seat and I will not score it.

D6 (venue_fit_and_contribution): This asks whether the work fits the configured venue and offers an original, significant contribution for that readership; it is owned by the editor-in-chief seat and I will not score it.

## Scoring Plan

### D4: cross_disciplinary_relevance
dimension_id: D4
what_to_look_for: Plain-language definitions of the core methods and of index nesting, an explicit translation of findings into portfolio-risk, hedging, diversification, or index/ETF design consequences, and implication claims that are tied to reported evidence with stated scope limits.
what_triggers_block: Practical, portfolio, or policy implications are a headline claim yet are asserted without any supporting quantitative translation, or core constructs are left undefined so an adjacent-field reader cannot interpret the main result.
what_triggers_warn: Implications are plausible but only partly substantiated, jargon or method definitions are uneven for non-specialist readers, or generalisation beyond the studied market is stated without adequate caveats.
